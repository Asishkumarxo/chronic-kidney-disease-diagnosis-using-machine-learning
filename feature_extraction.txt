Feature Extraction and Engineering Process
Machine Learning-Based Chronic Kidney Disease Prediction and Clinical Staging System

================================================================================
INTRODUCTION AND FEATURE TAXONOMY
================================================================================

In machine learning-based clinical decision support systems, the quality and 
representation of input features directly determine the model's predictive 
power, generalizability, and clinical utility. For the NephroAI system, 
features are extracted, processed, and engineered from standard patient health 
profiles, vitals, and routine laboratory panels.

The raw dataset contains 35 attributes (34 features and 1 target class). These 
attributes are classified into five clinical domains, spanning demographic info, 
hemodynamic vitals, urinalysis, blood chemistry, and clinical history:

1. Demographic & Vitals (6 Features)
   * Age: Patient age in years.
   * Gender: Patient sex (internally coded as 1 for Male, 0 for Female).
   * BMI: Body Mass Index (kg/m²), indicating metabolic and obesity status.
   * Systolic_BP: Systolic Blood Pressure (mmHg).
   * Diastolic_BP: Diastolic Blood Pressure (mmHg).
   * Heart_Rate: Resting heart rate (beats per minute).

2. Urinalysis Parameters (4 Features)
   * Urine_Albumin: Albumin excretion in urine (mg/dL), key marker of glomerular damage.
   * Urine_Protein: Total protein in urine (mg/dL), indicating proteinuria.
   * Albumin_Creatinine_Ratio (ACR): Urinary albumin to creatinine ratio (mg/g), the
     clinical gold-standard for detecting microalbuminuria.
   * Urine_Specific_Gravity: The ratio of urine density to water density, reflecting 
     the kidney's concentration ability.

3. Blood Chemistry / Renal Panel (10 Features used in model, 1 Excluded)
   * Serum_Creatinine: Concentration of creatinine in blood (mg/dL), primary kidney waste.
   * Blood_Urea_Nitrogen (BUN): Nitrogenous waste product in blood (mg/dL).
   * Sodium: Serum sodium concentration (mEq/L), reflects fluid balance.
   * Potassium: Serum potassium concentration (mEq/L), critical for cardiac function.
   * Calcium: Serum calcium levels (mg/dL), reflects mineral metabolism.
   * Phosphorus: Serum phosphorus levels (mg/dL), elevates in advanced renal disease.
   * Chloride: Serum chloride concentration (mEq/L), acid-base balance marker.
   * Bicarbonate: Carbon dioxide concentration as bicarbonate (mEq/L), flags metabolic acidosis.
   * Serum_Albumin: Concentration of albumin protein in serum (g/dL), nutritional/filter marker.
   * Total_Protein: Total protein in blood serum (g/dL).
   * eGFR (Excluded): Estimated Glomerular Filtration Rate (mL/min/1.73m²).

4. Hematology / Complete Blood Count (5 Features)
   * Hemoglobin: Oxygen-carrying protein in red blood cells (g/dL), flags CKD anemia.
   * RBC_Count: Red Blood Cell count (million cells/mcL).
   * WBC_Count: White Blood Cell count (cells/mcL), indicates inflammation/infection.
   * Platelet_Count: Platelet count (cells/mcL), reflects coagulation function.
   * Packed_Cell_Volume (PCV): Hematocrit percentage (%), proportion of blood volume.

5. Metabolic & Clinical History (9 Features)
   * Blood_Glucose_Random: Non-fasting blood sugar level (mg/dL).
   * Fasting_Glucose: Fasting blood glucose level (mg/dL).
   * HbA1c: Glycated hemoglobin percentage (%), indicating 3-month glycemic control.
   * Cholesterol: Total serum cholesterol (mg/dL).
   * Triglycerides: Serum triglyceride levels (mg/dL).
   * Diabetes: Medical history of diabetes (categorical: Yes/No).
   * Hypertension: Medical history of high blood pressure (categorical: Yes/No).
   * Smoking_Status: Current smoking behavior (categorical: Yes/No).
   * Family_History_Kidney: Hereditary kidney disease background (categorical: Yes/No).

================================================================================
PREVENTATIVE FEATURE ENGINEERING: DATA LEAKAGE MITIGATION
================================================================================

One of the most critical feature engineering decisions in NephroAI is the 
explicit exclusion of the eGFR (estimated Glomerular Filtration Rate) column 
from the model training feature set.

1. The Leakage Mechanism
   In nephrology, CKD stages are mathematically defined based on eGFR ranges 
   (e.g., Stage 5 is defined as eGFR < 15, Stage 4 as eGFR 15-29, etc.). Since 
   eGFR is calculated using deterministic clinical equations (like CKD-EPI) 
   that map directly to the target labels, leaving eGFR in the training set 
   would lead to a "cheating" model. The classifier would simply learn to 
   split the eGFR values at the predefined staging thresholds, ignoring all 
   other clinical biomarkers.

2. Generalizability and Clinical Logic
   By dropping the pre-computed eGFR column (via `df.drop(columns=['eGFR'])`), 
   the machine learning pipeline is forced to construct multi-dimensional decision 
   boundaries based on raw biomarkers (Serum Creatinine, Blood Urea Nitrogen, 
   Urine Albumin, Hemoglobin, Electrolytes, etc.). 
   This ensures that the model:
   * Learns complex biological interactions and correlation patterns.
   * Remains robust to clinical scenarios where the eGFR has not been 
     pre-calculated or is missing.
   * Serves as an independent cross-reference system alongside traditional 
     formula-based GFR estimation.

================================================================================
PIPELINE-EMBEDDED PREPROCESSING AND TRANSFORMATION
================================================================================

Raw features cannot be fed directly into machine learning classifiers due to 
differences in scales, units, missing values, and data formats. NephroAI 
utilizes a scikit-learn `ColumnTransformer` embedded within a `Pipeline` to 
extract and transform raw inputs into clean numerical vectors at both 
training and inference times.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
1. Numeric Feature Pipeline (29 Features)
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
For all continuous clinical variables (Age, Blood Pressures, Lab Biomarkers):

* Step 1: Missing Value Imputation (SimpleImputer)
  - Method: Median Imputation.
  - Rationale: Clinical biomarkers often contain outliers (e.g., highly elevated 
    creatinine levels). Imputing missing values using the median is robust to 
    these extreme outliers, preventing skewed distributions that would result 
    from mean-based imputation.
  - Implementation: `SimpleImputer(strategy='median')`

* Step 2: Z-Score Standardization (StandardScaler)
  - Method: Standard Scaling.
  - Rationale: Numerical features are measured on widely different scales. 
    For example, RBC Count ranges from 2.0 to 6.0, whereas WBC Count ranges 
    from 3,000 to 15,000. Algorithms like Logistic Regression are highly 
    sensitive to feature scales. Standard scaling centers each feature to 
    mean = 0 and scales to variance = 1.
  - Formula: z = (x - mean) / std
  - Implementation: `StandardScaler()`

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
2. Categorical Feature Pipeline (5 Features)
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
For qualitative nominal variables (Gender, Diabetes, Hypertension, etc.):

* Step 1: Missing Value Imputation (SimpleImputer)
  - Method: Mode Imputation.
  - Rationale: Missing categorical attributes are filled with the most frequent 
    class code (e.g., "No" for diabetes history if unspecified, or male gender).
  - Implementation: `SimpleImputer(strategy='most_frequent')`

* Step 2: One-Hot Encoding (OneHotEncoder)
  - Method: One-Hot categorical expansion.
  - Rationale: Categorical text strings (e.g., "Yes", "No") cannot be processed 
    mathematically. One-hot encoding creates a binary indicator column for 
    each unique category, preventing the model from assuming a false ordinal 
    relationship (which would occur if using simple label integer encoding).
  - Feature Expansion mapping:
    - Gender (0/1)           -> Gender_0 (Female), Gender_1 (Male)
    - Diabetes (Yes/No)      -> Diabetes_No, Diabetes_Yes
    - Hypertension (Yes/No)  -> Hypertension_No, Hypertension_Yes
    - Smoking_Status (Yes/No)-> Smoking_Status_No, Smoking_Status_Yes
    - Family_History_Kidney  -> Family_History_Kidney_No, Family_History_Kidney_Yes
  - Implementation: `OneHotEncoder(handle_unknown='ignore', sparse_output=False)`

================================================================================
DYNAMIC RUNTIME FEATURE EXTRACTION: CLINICAL EGFR
================================================================================

While eGFR is excluded from the machine learning model to prevent data leakage, 
an independent GFR value is dynamically extracted and calculated in the backend 
application at runtime. This extracted feature is used as a clinical reference 
standard on the patient dashboard.

1. Extraction Equation (2021 CKD-EPI Creatinine Equation)
   NephroAI implements the gold-standard, race-free 2021 CKD-EPI formula:

   GFR = 142 * min(Cr/k, 1)^alpha * max(Cr/k, 1)^(-1.200) * (0.9938)^age * sex_factor

   Where:
   * Cr: Serum Creatinine (mg/dL)
   * k: 0.9 for Males, 0.7 for Females
   * alpha: -0.302 for Males, -0.241 for Females
   * sex_factor: 1.000 for Males, 1.012 for Females
   * age: Patient age in years

2. Clinical Interpretation of Extracted GFR
   The calculated GFR value is mapped to standard KDIGO clinical categories:
   * GFR >= 90         : Normal or high kidney function (Stage 1)
   * GFR 60 - 89       : Mildly decreased kidney function (Stage 2)
   * GFR 30 - 59       : Moderately decreased kidney function (Stage 3)
   * GFR 15 - 29       : Severely decreased kidney function (Stage 4)
   * GFR < 15          : Kidney failure (Stage 5)

================================================================================
PATIENT-SPECIFIC RISK FEATURE EXTRACTION (EXPLAINABLE AI)
================================================================================

To transform the "black-box" model predictions into explainable clinical insights, 
NephroAI performs a dynamic patient-specific feature contribution extraction. 
This process identifies which abnormal lab parameters contributed most heavily 
to a high-risk prediction for that specific patient.

1. Global Feature Weighting
   At startup, the system extracts the global feature importances from the 
   trained Random Forest classifier. Since categorical variables are split during 
   one-hot encoding, the importances of the resulting sub-features are summed 
   to reconstruct the importance of the original base categorical feature:
   
   Importance(Base_Cat) = sum( Importance(One_Hot_Sub_Features) )

2. Clinical Threshold Bounds
   The system defines standard clinical threshold limits for 12 key features:
   * Serum_Creatinine: Upper bound = 1.2 mg/dL
   * Blood_Urea_Nitrogen: Upper bound = 20.0 mg/dL
   * Urine_Protein: Upper bound = 0.0 mg/dL (any protein is abnormal)
   * Urine_Albumin: Upper bound = 30.0 mg/dL
   * Albumin_Creatinine_Ratio: Upper bound = 30.0 mg/g
   * Systolic_BP: Upper bound = 130.0 mmHg
   * Diastolic_BP: Upper bound = 80.0 mmHg
   * HbA1c: Upper bound = 6.0%
   * Fasting_Glucose: Upper bound = 100.0 mg/dL
   * Cholesterol: Upper bound = 200.0 mg/dL
   * Hemoglobin: Lower bound = 12.0 g/dL
   * Bicarbonate: Lower bound = 22.0 mEq/L

3. Patient Deviation Score Calculation
   For each key biomarker, the patient's individual measurement is compared 
   against these clinical bounds to calculate a relative deviation:
   * If value > Upper Bound:  Deviation = (value - Upper) / Upper
   * If value < Lower Bound:  Deviation = (Lower - value) / Lower
   * Otherwise (normal):      Deviation = 0.0

4. Contribution Score and Feature Ranking
   The contribution of a specific abnormal feature to the patient's risk is 
   modeled as the product of their physiological deviation and the model's 
   global feature importance:
   
   Contribution_Score = Deviation_Score * Global_Feature_Importance

   All features with a Deviation_Score > 0 are ranked in descending order of 
   their Contribution_Score. The top 4 highest-scoring abnormal features are 
   extracted and returned as the patient's primary risk factors.

================================================================================
GLOBAL FEATURE IMPORTANCE EXTRACTION
================================================================================

The Random Forest model extracts global feature importances by calculating the 
mean decrease in impurity (Gini importance) across all decision trees. This 
represents the relative power of each biomarker in separating healthy kidneys 
from different stages of CKD.

The top 10 globally ranked features extracted from the trained model:

1. Serum_Creatinine (Highest Importance)
   * Rationale: The primary metabolic waste product cleared by the kidneys. 
     Rising levels are the most direct chemical indicator of deteriorating 
     nephron filtration capacity.
2. Blood_Urea_Nitrogen (BUN)
   * Rationale: Reflects urea accumulation in blood, signifying impaired 
     glomerular filtration and renal clearance.
3. Urine_Albumin
   * Rationale: Presence of albumin in urine (microalbuminuria) is the 
     cardinal sign of glomerular membrane barrier damage.
4. Hemoglobin
   * Rationale: Kidneys produce erythropoietin (EPO) to stimulate RBC production. 
     Deteriorating kidneys reduce EPO synthesis, leading to progressive anemia.
5. Urine_Protein
   * Rationale: Denotes macroproteinuria, indicating severe damage to the 
     glomerular filtration barriers.
6. Albumin_Creatinine_Ratio (ACR)
   * Rationale: Corrects urine albumin concentration for urine dilution, 
     offering a highly stable measure of protein excretion.
7. Bicarbonate
   * Rationale: Kidneys maintain acid-base balance; failure leads to retention 
     of hydrogen ions, resulting in low bicarbonate (metabolic acidosis).
8. Packed_Cell_Volume (PCV)
   * Rationale: Closely tracks hemoglobin; decreases in hematocrit reflect 
     the anemia of chronic kidney disease.
9. HbA1c
   * Rationale: Reflects long-term glycemic control. Poor diabetic control is the 
     leading cause of diabetic nephropathy (CKD).
10. Fasting_Glucose
    * Rationale: Tracks metabolic risk and acute diabetic stress, which are major 
      drivers of secondary kidney damage.
